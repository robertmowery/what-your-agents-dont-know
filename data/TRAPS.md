# The traps in the Tessaway Freight warehouse

Tessaway Freight is a fictional company and this data is synthetic. It is generated by `tessaway/warehouse/generate.py` from a fixed seed (`20261009`).

Nothing in the data is broken. Every table is complete, every key resolves, and the tests in `tests/test_generator.py` prove it. What the tables do not carry is meaning. Each gap below is a pattern common in freight data, planted on purpose.

This file, `data/DEFINITIONS.md` and `questions/part1.yaml` were committed before any agent ran. The traps are not tuned and no question is dropped after seeing agent results.

## What the warehouse models

Three source systems landed in one BigQuery dataset, the way a real warehouse looks:

| Prefix | Source system | Tables |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `tms_` | Transportation management | `tms_load_hdr`, `tms_stop`, `tms_stat_evt`, `tms_fac`, `tms_carr` |
| `bil_` | Carrier billing | `bil_cust_inv`, `bil_pod_doc`, `bil_carr_pay`, `bil_fuel_schg`, `bil_accsrl_chrg`, `bil_cr_memo`, `bil_canc_fee` |
| `ptl_` | Customer portal | `ptl_shpmt` |
| `mdm_`, `ref_` | Master and reference data | `mdm_cust_acct`, `ref_fx_rate` |

Loads are booked from January 2 through September 30, 2026. The warehouse is a snapshot taken at 06:00 UTC on October 1, 2026: about 11,500 load rows, 78,000 status events and 9,600 invoices.

Table and column names are abbreviated the way warehouse names usually are. None is deliberately misleading: `test_flg` marks test loads, `curr_cd` holds the currency, `tz_nm` holds the time zone.

## The six traps

The "naive" figures below come from the decoy SQL stored with each question. They were computed on BigQuery before any agent ran and are saved in `results/part1/gold_answers.json`.

### 1. On-time

Three systems hold three meanings.

| Meaning | Rule | September 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operations | Arrived at the final stop inside the appointment window | 84.41% |
| Customer contract (official) | Same, but late loads the shipper delayed are left out | 87.44% |
| Customer portal | Delivered on or before the requested date | 91.52% |

Where it is planted: appointment windows on `tms_stop`, the requested date on `tms_load_hdr.req_dlvr_dt`, the portal's own flag on `ptl_shpmt.on_time_ind`, and shipper-caused delays as `DLAY` events with reason `SHPR` in `tms_stat_evt`. About 28% of late loads have one.

Questions: M1, R1, R5.

### 2. Delivered

A status event says a load is delivered. Billing waits for a signed proof of delivery.

Where it is planted: a delivered load gets status `DLVD` when the carrier reports it. A row in `bil_pod_doc` arrives hours to weeks later, 5% of first documents arrive unsigned, and 2% never arrive. The invoice is cut the day a signed document is received. Of 1,097 loads delivered in September, 142 could not yet be billed at the snapshot.

Questions: R2, and every revenue and margin question (M2, M3, M4, E1, E3, R3).

### 3. Margin per load

Revenue minus carrier pay is the obvious reading, and the load table has a column for each. The rest of the margin sits in four other tables.

| Reading | Q3 2026 margin per load |
| --- | --- |
| Linehaul revenue minus linehaul cost on the load table | $437.37 |
| Invoice total minus carrier linehaul pay | $853.73 |
| Official: all revenue less all carrier cost, in US dollars | $409.19 |

Where it is planted: fuel surcharges in `bil_fuel_schg`, accessorials in `bil_accsrl_chrg`, credits in `bil_cr_memo` and canceled-load fees in `bil_canc_fee`. Each has a customer side and a carrier side. Canceled, test, voided and re-tendered rows in `tms_load_hdr` still carry linehaul amounts that were never billed.

Questions: M2, M3, M4, R6.

### 4. Customer

The shipper, the bill-to party and the parent account are three different rows in `mdm_cust_acct`.

Where it is planted: 39 parent accounts, 71 shipper sites and 32 bill-to parties. A load carries the shipper site and the bill-to party, never the parent. Northgate Foods Group ships from six sites, two of which do not carry its name (Harlow Dairy Co., Sunmeadow Bakeries). A freight payment firm, Ledgerline, is the bill-to party for nine customers and ships nothing.

| Reading of "largest customer by revenue, Q3 2026" | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Largest bill-to party | Ledgerline Freight Payment - Client Remit A |
| Largest shipper row | Pellam Paper - Savannah Mill |
| Official: largest parent account | Northgate Foods Group |

Questions: E1, E2, E3, E5.

### 5. A load

A row in `tms_load_hdr` is a tender, not a load.

Where it is planted: 576 loads were re-tendered after the first carrier fell off, so each appears twice, linked by `orig_load_id`, with the first row left at status `RTND`. 139 rows are test loads (`test_flg`) and 237 are voided (`void_flg`). None is deleted. Test loads run through every status, including delivered. September has 1,280 load rows and 1,170 loads.

Questions: E4, and every count of loads (M5, M6, E5, R2, R4).

### 6. Money and time

Cross-border loads carry Canadian dollars. Timestamps are stored in UTC and appointments in local time.

Where it is planted: 546 load rows are rated in Canadian dollars, with every billing amount for them stored unconverted beside a `curr_cd`. Monthly rates are in `ref_fx_rate`. Every `*_ts` column is UTC. Appointment windows (`appt_start_lcl`, `appt_end_lcl`) are wall-clock times at the facility, whose zone is in `tz_nm`. Compare the two directly and September's on-time rate comes out at 3.27%. Count deliveries by the UTC date and an evening delivery lands on the next day.

Questions: R3, R4, M1, R1, and every money question.

## Questions with no trap, and questions where the trap is small

The six lookup questions (L1 to L6) need no definition. They are the control group.

Two other questions turned out forgiving once the data was built, and they stay in the set as written:

- E6 (the outside carrier that delivered the most loads) has a clear leader on any reading.
- M5 (loads delivered in September) is 1,097 on the official definition and 1,109 counting delivered events by UTC date with test loads included. It is scored exactly, as a count, so the naive count is still wrong, but only by twelve.

## One calibration, made before any agent ran

The first build of the data put the naive margin per load ($441.89) within one percent of the official figure ($444.97): fuel and accessorial margin happened to cancel credits. A trap that scores the naive answer as right is not a trap, so two generator settings were changed: credit memos went from 3% to 5% of invoices with larger amounts, and carriers now receive 97% to 102% of the fuel surcharge billed. One question was also reworded at that point: R6 first asked for canceled-load fees billed, which had no Canadian-dollar rows in the quarter, and now asks for margin on canceled loads. Both changes were made by comparing gold SQL with decoy SQL. No agent had been run, and nothing has been changed since.
